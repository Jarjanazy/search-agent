# Syrian Olive Oil: Weekly Business Intelligence Report

## Executive Summary

Syria's 2026 olive harvest is forecast at 412,000 tons of olives yielding only 65,000 tons of oil—an average-to-weak season driven by recurring drought and declining rainfall. The Ministry of Agriculture authorized 10,000 tons of olive oil exports in October 2024, though actual exports remain limited; Al Reef Company, a major exporter, shipped only 500 tons last year primarily to Gulf markets and cautioned it cannot predict 2026 export volumes. Water scarcity is the sector's critical constraint, with olive cultivation requiring approximately 5,000 cubic meters of water per ton produced, while coastal regions have experienced 50% production losses over two decades. International cooperation through FAO, Italy, and the "One Country One Priority Product" initiative aims to revive the sector through value chain analysis and infrastructure rehabilitation.

## Key Findings

### Production

- **2026 Forecast:** Ministry of Agriculture anticipates 412,000 tons of olives to yield 65,000 tons of oil, classified as average-to-weak season (Director Abeer Jawhar, Olive Bureau)
- **2024 Season (Most Recent Complete Year):** 122,000 tons of oil produced; 100,000 tons for domestic consumption; ~22,000 tons surplus for export
- **Historic Comparison:** Production peaked at nearly one million tons of olives annually before 2011 conflict; ranged 113,230–125,000 tons oil equivalent in recent years
- **Regional Production Breakdown (2024 season estimates):**
  - Idlib: Over 100,000 tons of olives annually (largest single region)
  - Hama: 91,000 tons of olives (highest production in government-controlled areas)
  - Homs: 82,755 tons of olives
  - Latakia: 47,556 tons of olives
  - Damascus countryside: 40,277 tons of olives
  - Tartous and Latakia combined: 11.2% of national cultivation (coastal, rain-fed varieties)
  - Aleppo (especially Afrin region): Major producer; 56% of national cultivation shared with Idlib
- **Long-term Trend:** Reported annual decline of 1.3% forecast through 2026; production fell from seventh-largest globally (2021) toward smaller share
- **Daraa Region Crisis:** Harvest declined 68% year-on-year (2025) and 86% versus 2011; yield per tree halved; farms with previous 20-tank-season output near zero production

### Prices & Trade

- **Export Pricing:** Year-on-year increase from approximately $6 USD per liter (2024) to over $8 USD per liter (2025 forecast)
- **Wholesale Pricing Dynamics:** Reported 100% price doubling within three months due to Gulf exports; Al Reef Company bases pricing on press-purchase rates
- **Export Volumes:** Al Reef Company (major exporter) shipped 500 tons in 2024 to Gulf countries; stated inability to predict 2026 volumes
- **Export Authorization:** October 2024 Ministry of Economy decision permits 10,000 tons annual export (licensed companies only, filtered and packaged in containers ≤5 liters)
- **Export History:** Government ban imposed September 2023 (lifted December 2023 for 5,000 tons); re-authorized at 10,000 tons in October 2024
- **Active Markets:** Gulf countries (primary 2024 market); Europe historically minimal (EU imports 0.15 million units historically); Saudi Arabia identified as growing market
- **Trade Restrictions:** EU market access limited by established protection barriers favoring Spain, Italy, Greece; European imports 2025/26 show Syria at 1,699 metric tons of 56,702 total third-country imports

### Quality & Standards

- **International Olive Council:** No recent compliance failures reported; Syria operates within IOC trade standards framework (global oil production 2025/26 estimated 2,986,000 tonnes)
- **Certification Progress:** No new IOC certifications or standard failures documented in current data; Fairtrade International operates certification framework for Palestinian olive oil but no current Fairtrade Syrian oil certifications identified
- **Processing Methods:** Syrian mills employ centrifugal cold extraction systems (temperatures maintained below 27°C for quality extra virgin oils); malaxer technology in widespread use; cold-press terminology marketing standard but no pressing involved in modern extraction
- **Mill Infrastructure:** Presses operational across Aleppo, coastal regions (Tartous, Latakia), and Homs; no recent mill rehabilitation data beyond FAO irrigation infrastructure projects

### Market Opportunities

- **FAO Value Chain Initiative:** FAO, Italian Government, and Syrian Ministry of Agriculture launched "One Country One Priority Product" roundtable (February 2025) to present comprehensive value chain study; identified olives as national priority with 700,000 hectares under cultivation supporting 300,000 farming families
- **Italian Cooperation:** Italian Ambassador participation signals Italian government interest in agricultural knowledge transfer and sector development partnerships
- **FAO Infrastructure Programs:** "Building Local Resilience in Syria" (BLRS) project funded by UK Aid rehabilitating critical irrigation infrastructure in Aleppo Governorate; restoration reduced water losses across thousands of hectares; 3,970 smallholder farmers benefited
- **Water Management Technology:** FAO promoting smart irrigation systems adoption to improve production efficiency and sustainability
- **European Market Entry:** Fairtrade certification noted as most-used ethical framework for Palestinian oils; potential model for Syrian market access if pursued; CBI identifies strict EU testing and labeling requirements as barriers and opportunity for certified producers
- **Emerging Buyer Interest:** Saudi Arabia identified as developing market; 2026 consumption projected at 23,010 metric tons with limited domestic production
- **Cold-Press Marketing:** Growing consumer demand for authentic cold-pressed extra virgin oils; Syrian coastal and Afrin varieties recognized for quality

### Risks & Disruptions

- **Severe Water Scarcity:** Producing one ton of olives requires ~5,000 cubic meters of water; declining rainfall and expansion of irrigated areas increased reliance on blue water (groundwater wells); proliferation of illegal shallow-well drilling in rain-fed regions (Aleppo, coastal areas) increasing drought risk
- **Coastal Production Collapse:** Olive production in coastal regions declined 50% over past two decades; Latakia and Tartous rain-fed cultivation severely affected by recurring droughts
- **Regional Production Vulnerability:** 40% of Syrian olive trees located outside regime control (rural Aleppo and Idlib), creating supply security and control fragmentation; Kurdish-controlled northeastern regions experience competing political claims over water infrastructure
- **Daraa Agricultural Crisis:** Southern region harvest 68% lower than 2024; production near zero in drought-affected areas despite historical 75,800 tons annual pre-conflict output; farmers abandoning cultivated fields
- **Conflict and Security:** January–February 2026 military operations in northeastern Syria near dam areas; Kurdish-regime military clashes affecting agricultural regions; fighting near critical water infrastructure (dam area)
- **Climate Shocks:** Extended drought persists; 2026 harvest forecast emphasizes "average-to-weak" outlook despite slight recovery from 2025
- **Water Infrastructure Degradation:** Syria lacks critical irrigation construction; wastewater treatment systems insufficient; 14+ years of conflict damaged networks; rehabilitation underway but incomplete
- **Domestic Consumption Priority:** 2024 domestic consumption at 100,000 tons with only 22,000 tons surplus; 2026 forecast of 65,000 tons oil threatens export quantities if consumption targets unchanged
- **Exporter Uncertainty:** Al Reef Company unable to predict 2026 export volumes despite authorization; signals market unpredictability and supply chain fragility
- **Export Policy Volatility:** Multiple export ban cycles (September 2023, December 2023, October 2024) demonstrate unstable policy environment; new Syrian government (post-2025 political changes) strategy for olive exports unconfirmed
